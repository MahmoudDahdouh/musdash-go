# Updates of 2026-10-11

Seven things asked for on 2026-10-11. Each was planned, the plan reviewed, built, and the code reviewed, in that order; what the reviews changed is said under each. They were built in the order asked.

What all seven share: nothing new is imported, nothing is kept in memory between requests, and no page gains an inline script or style.

## 1. A Back button

**Asked:** a back button.

**What was there:** the bar at the top has the trail (`Default team ⌄ / Projects / Shop ⌄ / production ⌄ / web ⌄`). The steps that are switchers open a menu and are not links, so from an app's page the only link upward in the trail is "Projects": the way to the environment is inside a menu.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One button, in the bar, before the trail: an arrow and, from 64rem up, the word "Back". It is drawn by `ui.Layout`, so every page has it or not by one rule | A page's header is drawn by a dozen different templates; the bar is drawn once |
| 2 | It is a link to the place above this one: the `Href` of the nearest step before the last that has one. `projectCrumb`, the environment's step and `resourceCrumb` get an `Href`, the page of the place they name (the trail still draws them as switchers) | Without script, with a middle click and for a page opened from a link in a message, Back has to lead somewhere sensible: up |
| 3 | `app.js` makes it a real Back when there is one: the page was come to from another page of this dashboard (`document.referrer` has this origin and another address, and is not a signed-out page) and the tab has history. Then `history.back()`, which also puts a person back where a list was scrolled to | "Back" means where I was. A person who went Add resource → a card's form expects the list again, at their place, not the environment's page |
| 4 | A page with nothing above it (Home, Projects, Servers, every page of the sidebar) has no button | There is nowhere to go; the sidebar is the way between those |
| 5 | A click with a modifier key, or the middle button, is the link's own | It opens the place above in a new tab, as any link does |

**Review of the plan.** Two cases the first draft got wrong. A page that answers its own form (Settings saved, a redirect to itself) has itself as referrer: `history.back()` would show the same page again and look broken, so the same address is not "somewhere else" and the link is followed. And a person who has just signed in has `/login` as referrer: going back there bounces them to Home; signed-out addresses do not count either.

| 6 | A page that is not the place its trail ends at says where Back leads (`ui.Shell.Back`, set with `under(shell, path)`): one deployment leads to the Deployments tab, a task to Tasks, the developer view of the variables to Environment | Every page of an app has the app's trail. Without this, Back from a deployment would skip the list of deployments |

**Tests:** `TestBackButtonLeadsUp`: an app's page has the button leading to its environment; a deployment's to the Deployments tab; an environment's to its project; Add resource's to the environment, and a card's form to Add resource; a server's to the list of servers; Home, Projects and the other pages of the sidebar have none.

**Review of the code.** Decision 6 came out of reading the handlers: every tab and sub-page of an app shares one trail, so the trail alone sent a deployment's Back to the environment. Tried in a browser: Add resource scrolled to its third page, a card's form opened, Back pressed: the list again, on its third page, scrolled to the same card (3754px). Opened directly, the same button leads to Add resource's start.

## 2. The field and the Category menu stay in sight on Add resource

**Asked:** on Add resource, the search field and the category filter are sticky when the page is scrolled.

**What was there:** the row is the first thing under the page's title and scrolls away with it; the list under it is several hundred cards long.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The row is `position: sticky` under the bar (`.sticky-bar`, `top` the bar's height, which becomes a token, `--size-topbar`), on the page's own colour, above the cards | Asked for |
| 2 | While it is stuck it has a line under it (`data-stuck`, set by `app.js`; see the review of the code for how it is told) | Cards that slide under an edge nobody can see look cut off. CSS alone cannot tell a stuck element in every browser yet |
| 3 | On one line at every width: the field takes what the menu and Clear filters leave; below 40rem Clear filters is its icon alone, with its name as a label | A sticky row of two lines would take a fifth of a phone's screen |
| 4 | When the list is asked for again while the row is stuck, the page goes to the list's start (`htmx:afterSwap` on `#kinds`) | New results start at the top. Without it the page stays scrolled where the old list was, which in a shorter list is its end or nothing |
| 5 | The Category menu stays open through that: the scroll that the new list causes is not the person's (`settling`, which already covers the frames after a tick, now also covers a swap of the list) | A person ticking three categories would have the menu shut after each |

**Review of the plan.** The next page of cards (`.offers-more`) also swaps into the list; it must not send the page to the top. It swaps the row itself (`outerHTML` of the `<li>`), not `#kinds`, so the target tells them apart: only a swap whose target is `#kinds` scrolls.

**Tests:** `TestAddResourceFiltersStayInSight`: the row is the sticky one, names its list and holds the field, the menu and Clear filters, and the list is the element after it. Two older assertions counted the words "Clear filters", which the button now carries as its label too; they count the links to the bare address.

**Review of the code.** Decision 2 changed while it was tried: an `IntersectionObserver` is not told anything in a page that is not being drawn, and at the place where the row is exactly back where it belongs it could not tell stuck from not. The row is the first thing in its parent, so it is stuck while it stands lower in the parent than its margin puts it: two boxes read at every scroll, and the same question the swap asks. Tried in a browser: scrolled to 2500px with the row stuck, Analytics ticked in the menu: the menu stayed open, the page went to the list's start (92px, the row back in its place with its 20px above the list) and showed 44 services from the first. At 375px the row is one line; the menu's button was cut to "Categ…" until the menu and the button were told not to give way.

## 3. The search finds what can be deployed

**Asked:** the search in the bar also searches the resources that can be added, like n8n. Clicking one deploys it: the person chooses the project, then the environment.

**What was there:** the search finds what the team has (projects, environments, apps, databases, services, domains, servers, tags) and the pages of the sidebar. What can be added is found only on an environment's Add resource page.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | With something typed, `GET /search` also answers with the database engines and service templates whose name (or key) holds every word, ranked as everything else is by where the word is in the name, at most six of them (`searchOffers`) | The catalogue is several hundred templates; a search for "a" must stay the team's own things first |
| 2 | Such a row has the product's logo, "Service template" or "Database", and a **Deploy** badge where a state would be | It must not be mistaken for something the team runs |
| 3 | It leads to `GET /deploy/{kind}/{key}` (`service` or `database`; a Member's), a page that names the thing and asks where it goes: a Project `Select` and an Environment `Select`, and **Continue** | "Choose the project, then the env." Two menus with the likely answer already chosen is one click in the usual case and still a choice |
| 4 | Choosing a project asks `GET /deploy/environments?project=` for that project's environments (the Environment field is replaced) | The second list depends on the first |
| 5 | Continue is a `GET` of the same address with `project`, `env` and `go=1`: the server checks both belong to the team and to each other and redirects to the form the Add resource card leads to (`…/service/new?template=` or `…/database/new?engine=`) | The form that exists already asks for the name, the server and the domain, and has the Deploy button. Nothing is made by the chooser, so it needs no POST |
| 6 | Chosen at first: the project and environment of the page the search was opened from (`HX-Current-URL`, read as a hint and checked like any id), else the project something was last deployed in and its first environment | Searching from inside an environment usually means "here" |
| 7 | No project yet: the page says so and offers New project | Nothing to choose from |
| 8 | A key that is no template, or a kind that is neither, is 404. Another team's project is not found | As everywhere |

**Review of the plan.** The ways to make an app (Docker image, a repository) were in the first draft as rows too. They are left out: their cards depend on what the team has connected, and "like n8n" is the catalogue. Also: the row must not be a `Pill`, which is a state; it is a `Badge`.

**Tests:** a template is found by its name and leads to the chooser; nothing of the catalogue is listed with nothing typed; at most six; the chooser lists the team's projects and not another team's; Continue redirects to the form; an environment of another project, or another team's, is refused; an unknown key is 404; the environments answer follows the project.

## 4. What is deploying now, floating at the bottom end

**Asked:** while an app, database or service is deploying, a simple floating notification at the bottom end says what is deploying right now. Clicking it goes to the source of the deployment.

**What was there:** a deployment is seen on its own page, on its resource's header, and on Home's activity.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `db.Deploying(team, limit)`: one statement, three arms, the team's id in each. An app's arm reads the app's five newest deployments by the index there is (`deployments_app`) and takes the oldest that is queued or running; a service's the same of `service_deployments`; a database's is `status = 'deploying'` | No index on a deployment's status exists, and the table grows for ever: nothing here walks it. No migration |
| 2 | `ui.Deploying`, in the layout of every signed-in page, in the corner the toasts have (the same stack: a toast stands above it). One row a thing, at most three, then "and N more" to Home. A row is a link: a spinning icon, the name, and "Deploying", "Queued" or (a database) "Starting" | "Simple". Three rows is what fits a corner |
| 3 | A row leads to the deployment's own page (an app's, a service's), a database's to the database | "The source of deploying": where its log and stages are |
| 4 | It is in the page as the page is drawn (`s.shell` asks), and asks for itself at `GET /deploying` (`ui.Poll`: a 204 while nothing changed): every 3 seconds while it shows something, every 15 while it shows nothing | A person who pressed Deploy sees it on the page they land on. One that started elsewhere (a push, a colleague) shows within 15 seconds |
| 5 | That poll does not keep a session alive: the middleware does not extend a session for it | A tab left open would otherwise never sign out, on any page |
| 6 | When the query fails the page is drawn without it and the error is logged | A corner note must not take a page down |
| 7 | It is a `<nav aria-label="Deploying now">` whose list is polite live text | Announced when it changes, never focus-stealing |

**Review of the plan.** The first draft drew it as a card the width of a toast; on a phone that covers the page's last row. It is as wide as its text, at most 20rem, and the page already ends with 48px of space. And a preview's deployment is an app's deployment: it is listed, by the preview's own name.

**Tests:** nothing in progress draws no rows and asks every 15s; a queued app deployment, a running service deployment and a starting database are each listed with the right link; another team's are not; four in progress show three and "1 more"; the poll answers 204 for what the page holds; the poll does not extend the session.

## 5. Four cards at the top of Home

**Asked:** four cards at the top of Home, the ones a person really needs.

**What a person opens Home to find out**, in the order they ask it: is everything up; did my deployments work; has the server room left; is my data safe. A count of projects or domains answers none of them, so none is a card.

| # | Card | Figure | Flag | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Running** | "12 of 14" apps, databases and services | red "2 down" (failed, exited, degraded); green "All running" when every one runs | Projects |
| 2 | **Deployments** | how many in the last 24 hours (apps' and services') | red "1 failed"; blue "2 in progress" | Recent activity, on the page |
| 3 | **Servers** | "2 of 2" ready | red "1 unreachable"; amber or red when the fullest disk is over 75% or 90%. The line under says the fullest disk and whose | Servers |
| 4 | **Backups** | when the last one succeeded ("3 hours ago") | red "1 failed" (a database whose latest backup failed); amber "2 without a schedule" | the database whose backup failed, else Recent activity |

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ui.Figures` and `ui.Figure`: a grid of four (two on a tablet, one on a phone), each a card that is one link: icon and label, the figure, a flag, a line | A new component: `ui.Stat` is a cell of a strip inside a card, with a bar |
| 2 | All of it from the database, in `db.TeamTotals` (which gains Down, the deployments' counts and the backups') and the servers and samples Home already reads | Home asks no server anything |
| 3 | A local server counts as ready; a remote one by its last check | Home cannot ask Docker |
| 4 | The cards are inside `HomeLive`, so they follow a deployment in progress | They are the part that changes |
| 5 | An install with nothing deployed keeps Getting started and has no cards | Four zeros say nothing |
| 6 | No database: the Backups card says "No databases" and nothing else | Honest, and the place stays the same |

**Review of the plan.** "Needs attention" as a card of its own was weighed against Backups: it would repeat the red flags of the other three. Backups stays. The deployment count must not walk the whole table: it is asked per app through `deployments_app` with the day's start as the range.

**Tests:** the four cards with the figures of a seeded team; a failed app flags Running; a failed deployment flags Deployments; an unreachable server flags Servers; a failed latest backup flags Backups and leads to that database; an empty install has none; another team's rows are not counted.

## 6. Everything that loads says so

**Asked:** every loading process shows that it is loading: a skeleton for a card, component or section; a simple loader icon for a button.

**What was there:** a button whose htmx request is in flight is dimmed; a form that navigates shows nothing; menus and the search say "Loading…"; the containers of a stack say a sentence; a usage strip draws dashes.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Buttons.** A button that is waiting shows a turning ring: in place of its icon when it has one, else over its label, which keeps its width (Primer's own rule). It is marked `aria-busy` | The label stays readable where there is room, and nothing moves |
| 2 | Waiting is: an htmx request this button or its form made (`.htmx-request`, as now, but only on a button or a `form`, never on an element that polls); and a form that navigates, from `submit` until the page is left (`app.js` marks the button that sent it, `data-busy`; a page brought back by Back has none) | A plain form post is most of the dashboard's buttons, and had no feedback at all |
| 3 | **Skeletons.** `ui.Skeleton(kind)`: grey bars with a slow shimmer, `aria-hidden`, in a container marked `aria-busy`; still under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Kinds: `rows` (a list in a card), `menu` (options of a menu), `cards` (offers) and `text` | One component, four shapes, all from tokens |
| 4 | Where: a Picker's and a switcher's options and the search's list (were "Loading…"); the next Picker's list when a choice empties it (`app.js`); a stack's containers; the usage strip's pending figures; the list of Add resource while it is asked for again | Every place that draws before it has its content |
| 5 | A list that is asked for again keeps what it shows, dimmed, and turns into skeleton cards only after 300ms | A skeleton that flashes for 30ms on every letter typed is worse than none |
| 6 | The next page of cards: its button turns while it asks | It is a button |

**Review of the plan.** A header that polls (`ui.Poll`) is marked `htmx-request` for as long as each poll takes; with the old selector every submit button in it would show a ring every two seconds. The `cancel` branch narrows that selector to `form.htmx-request` for the same reason; this takes the same line so the two agree.

**Tests:** the skeleton renders hidden from a reader and its container busy; the menus hold no "Loading…" text; `TestSignedInPagesHaveNoInlineScriptOrStyle` still passes; the rest in a browser with a slowed connection.

## 7. Critical actions ask in red, with an icon

**Asked:** every critical action (delete, stop, …) asks in a dialog with a red button; Stop of a Compose stack asks in blue, which is wrong. And buttons have icons where one says something: Delete has the bin. Not every button needs one.

**What was there:** `ui.Ask(title, question, submit, danger)`: 22 of 36 uses passed `danger`, the rest asked with the primary blue: Stop (app, database, service), Restart (database, a stack's container), Roll back, Replace (deploy token, webhook secret), Change role, Reinstall proxy, Install proxy, Make reset link. Half the buttons that remove a row had no icon, and the dialog's own button never had one.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Red is for what takes something away or interrupts something that runs. That is every question but two: **Install proxy** and **Make reset link**, which add something | Asked for, and the two that stay blue would lie in red |
| 2 | `ui.Ask` keeps its shape; the ten uses change their last argument | The smallest change that cannot miss one, given the test below |
| 3 | The dialog's button shows the icon of the button that was pressed: `app.js` copies it (`.icon`) in front of the label | One source for the icon; no list of icons in script |
| 4 | So every button that asks has an icon: bin for Delete, Remove and Revoke; stop; refresh for Restart, Replace and Reinstall; a new `undo` for Roll back; `x` for Cancel invitation; and so on | "Always add icons for the button" |
| 5 | `ui.Confirm` (the typed name) and a `FormDialog` with `Danger` draw their red button with an icon too (`DialogProps.Icon`, bin by default) | The same rule for the dialogs that are not the shared one |
| 6 | `TestCriticalFormsAsk` also fails for a question that is not red unless it is one of the two named, and for a button that asks without an icon | So the next one cannot be blue by accident |

**Review of the plan.** A menu item that asks (a variable's Delete, a container's Restart, Sign out) has its icon already; Restart in a stack's row menu stays a plain item in the menu (it ends nothing) and asks in red. Small buttons in table rows get wider with an icon: checked that the rows that hold two of them still fit at 64rem.

**Tests:** the template test above; the page of a service asks to stop in red.
